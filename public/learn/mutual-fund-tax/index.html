How mutual funds are taxed

Tax on mutual funds depends on the type of fund and how long you hold it. Here are the rules for the 2026–27 financial year, in plain language.

Rules as of November 2026, for FY 2026–27. Tax rules change with each Budget; check the current rules or a tax professional before acting.

You pay tax when you sell

With growth plans, you pay capital gains tax only when you redeem (sell) units. The gain is the sale value minus the purchase cost. Switching from one fund to another, or from a Regular plan to a Direct plan, counts as a sale.

Equity funds

A fund counts as "equity-oriented" when at least 65% of it is in Indian shares.

  • Held 12 months or less: short-term gains are taxed at 20%.
  • Held more than 12 months: long-term gains are taxed at 12.5%, and the first ₹1.25 lakh of such gains in a financial year is tax-free.

The Union Budget in February 2026 left these rates unchanged.

Debt funds

For debt funds bought on or after 1 April 2023, all gains are added to your income and taxed at your slab rate, however long you hold them. There is no long-term rate. Units bought before that date follow older rules.

Each SIP instalment is separate

Every SIP instalment is a separate purchase with its own date. When you redeem, the oldest units go first, and each is short-term or long-term depending on its own holding period.

Using the ₹1.25 lakh exemption

Because the first ₹1.25 lakh of long-term equity gains each year is tax-free, some investors sell and reinvest part of their holdings each year to use the exemption. This is called tax harvesting. It needs care with exit loads and the holding period of the new units.

ELSS and the old tax regime

Investments in ELSS (equity-linked savings scheme) funds qualify for a deduction of up to ₹1.5 lakh a year, but only under the old tax regime. They are locked in for three years.

Other funds

Hybrid, international, gold, silver and fund-of-funds schemes each have their own rules, depending on what the fund holds. Check the treatment of a specific fund before investing.

In short

Equity funds: 20% if sold within a year, 12.5% after a year with ₹1.25 lakh a year exempt. Debt funds bought since April 2023: taxed at your slab rate.
