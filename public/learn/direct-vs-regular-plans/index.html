Direct vs Regular plans

Every mutual fund comes in two versions that hold exactly the same investments. The only difference is who gets paid, and it adds up over time.

Same fund, two price tags

Every mutual fund scheme offers a Regular plan and a Direct plan. Both are managed by the same fund manager and own exactly the same shares or bonds. The difference is the expense ratio, the annual charge the fund deducts from its assets.

A Regular plan's expense ratio includes a commission paid to the distributor who sold it to you. A Direct plan has no distributor, so its expense ratio is lower. Because the charge comes out of the fund every day, the Direct plan's NAV grows slightly faster, year after year.

Why a small difference matters

The gap in expense ratio is often around half a percentage point to one percentage point a year, depending on the fund. That sounds small, but it compounds: it is taken from your whole balance every year, including the gains from earlier years.

An illustration, not a forecast: suppose a fund earns 10% a year before charges, and its Direct plan charges 0.5% a year while its Regular plan charges 1.5%. Over 20 years, ₹10 lakh grows to about ₹61 lakh in the Direct plan and about ₹51 lakh in the Regular plan. The investments were identical; the ₹10 lakh difference was cost.

Are Regular plans ever right?

A Regular plan's commission pays for the distributor's service. If someone helps you with paperwork and keeps you invested through bad markets, that has value. The questions are whether you know what you are paying, and whether the person is advising you or selling to you.

A fee-only adviser charges you directly and recommends Direct plans, so the cost is visible and the advice isn't tied to commission. How fee-only advice works.

How to tell which plan you hold

  • Look at the scheme name on your account statement: it says "Direct" or "Regular".
  • Your consolidated account statement (CAS) lists every holding and its plan.
  • Switching from Regular to Direct counts as selling and buying, so it can trigger tax. Work out the tax before switching.

In short

Direct and Regular plans hold the same investments. Regular plans pay a distributor commission every year through a higher expense ratio; over long periods the difference compounds.
