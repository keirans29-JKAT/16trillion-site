What a fee-only investment adviser does

Who pays for financial advice decides whose interests it serves. Here is how a SEBI-registered, fee-only adviser works, and how that differs from a distributor.

Two very different jobs that look alike

In India, most people get mutual fund recommendations from a distributor: a bank relationship manager, an agent or an investment app. Distributors are paid a commission by the fund house, taken out of the fund's expenses every year for as long as you stay invested. The advice feels free, but you pay for it through your returns.

A SEBI-registered investment adviser (RIA) does a different job. An RIA is registered with the Securities and Exchange Board of India to give investment advice, and is paid only by the client, through a fee agreed in writing. An RIA cannot take commission from the products they recommend.

Why the payment model matters

When an adviser earns more from one product than another, even an honest adviser faces a pull in that direction. A fee-only adviser earns the same whichever fund you choose, so the only question left is which option suits you. That is why fee-only advisers recommend direct plans, which carry no distributor commission.

How advisers are allowed to charge

SEBI allows two ways to charge, and caps both:

  • A percentage of the assets advised on, up to 2.5% a year per family.
  • A fixed fee, up to ₹1,51,000 a year per family.

The fee, what it covers and how to end the agreement must be set out in a signed agreement before any advice is given.

What a good adviser actually does

  • Understands your situation first: income, family, goals, existing investments, loans and how much risk you can take.
  • Assesses your risk profile and checks every recommendation is suitable for it. What a risk profile means.
  • Builds a written plan and explains the reasoning behind each recommendation.
  • Reviews it regularly, and tells you when something material changes.
  • Keeps records of the advice and the reasons for it, as SEBI requires.

Questions worth asking any adviser

  • Are you registered with SEBI as an investment adviser? What is your registration number?
  • Do you or anyone connected to you earn commission from what you recommend?
  • How exactly is your fee worked out, and what does it cover?
  • How often will we review the plan?

You can check any adviser's registration on SEBI's website.

In short

A fee-only RIA is paid only by you, under a written agreement, and takes no commission. That removes the main conflict of interest in financial advice.
